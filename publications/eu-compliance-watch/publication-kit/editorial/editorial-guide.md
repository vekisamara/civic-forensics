# EU Compliance Watch — Editorial Guide v3.0

## Purpose

EU Compliance Watch converts European regulatory developments into verifiable civic oversight tools. It is not a general EU news feed and does not present legal advice.

## Publication families

### Deep Dive — `ECW-DD-xxx`
Reference analysis of one regulatory subject. Typical length: 3,000–5,000 words.

### Watch Report — `ECW-WR-xxx`
Time-sensitive analysis of a new decision, guideline, consultation or enforcement development. Typical length: 800–1,500 words.

### Citizen Guide — `ECW-CG-xxx`
Practical explanation of how citizens can use a legal or administrative mechanism.

### Citizen Checklist — `ECW-CL-xxx`
Compact, standalone verification list suitable for printing or field use.

## Four-phase workflow

1. **Research dossier** — official sources, chronology, legal basis, unresolved questions.
2. **Civic Forensics analysis** — evidence, verifiability, formalism risks, missing records.
3. **Publication** — edited public document with clear separation of fact and analysis.
4. **Companion package** — portal summary, SEO metadata, social copy, checklist and image brief.

## Source hierarchy

1. EU legislation and official consolidated texts.
2. Decisions, judgments, guidelines and official institutional documents.
3. Official registers, datasets and notices.
4. Academic and expert commentary.
5. Reputable journalism for context, not as the sole basis of a legal claim.

## Required distinctions

Every publication must distinguish:

- **Fact** — directly supported by a cited source.
- **Rule** — legal or regulatory requirement.
- **Interpretation** — reasoned reading of facts or rules.
- **Inference** — conclusion drawn from incomplete evidence and labelled as such.
- **Recommendation** — proposed action.
- **Unknown** — point that cannot be verified from available records.

## Evidence rule

An assertion of compliance is not evidence of compliance. Prefer records that show what happened: system logs, notices, contracts, review records, metadata, decision trails, approval authority, test results and dates.

## Language

Use plain English. Define legal and technical terms on first use. Avoid advocacy slogans inside the analytical body. Strong conclusions require strong evidence.

## Corrections and versioning

- `1.0` initial publication
- `1.1` minor correction or source update
- `1.2` material factual update without structural revision
- `2.0` substantial legal or methodological revision

Every change after publication must be recorded in the version history.

## Editorial status

Use one of: `Working draft`, `Editorial review`, `Published`, `Superseded`, `Withdrawn`.

## Quality-control questions

- Is every material legal claim tied to a primary source?
- Are event date and publication date distinguished?
- Are facts separated from inference?
- Is the practical relevance to civic oversight explicit?
- Can the reader identify the evidence needed to verify compliance?
- Are limitations and unknowns stated?
- Does the document preserve accessibility and citation integrity?
