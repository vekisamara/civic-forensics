# EU Compliance Watch — Citation Guide v3.0

## Recommended publication citation

`Civic Forensics, [Title], EU Compliance Watch, [Series] No. [ID], Version [x.x], [Month Year].`

Example:

`Civic Forensics, Article 50 Is Now Enforceable: A Civic Guide to AI Transparency, EU Compliance Watch, Deep Dive No. ECW-DD-001, Version 1.0, August 2026.`

## Legal sources

Use the official title, instrument number, relevant article or paragraph, issuing institution and stable official source.

## Web sources

Record institution or author, title, publication date, access date and stable URL. Preserve a local evidence copy when legally and technically appropriate.

## Judgments and decisions

Include court or authority, case identifier, parties or short title, decision date and paragraph numbers where available.

## Source notes

Do not cite a search-result snippet. Verify the underlying document. Distinguish publication date from the date of the event described.

## In-text style

Use concise footnotes for legal and documentary support. Avoid placing raw URLs in the body unless the document format requires them.

## Evidence archive naming

`YYYY-MM-DD_institution_short-title_document-id.ext`

## Citation integrity

A citation must support the exact sentence or paragraph to which it is attached. Sources should not be used to imply conclusions they do not contain.
