# CFP Editorial Guide v1.0

## Purpose

The Civic Forensics Portfolio (CFP) series presents the institutional capacity, programme architecture, results, partnership opportunities and strategic development of Civic Forensics — Independent Civic Intelligence Initiative.

## Editorial principles

1. Distinguish current achievements from planned development.
2. Use evidence before aspiration.
3. Avoid inflated institutional language.
4. State ownership, status, dates and evidence for each claimed result.
5. Separate public-interest analysis from fundraising language.
6. Describe cooperation opportunities without implying endorsement.
7. Use clear international English.
8. Design every document for accessibility, reuse and version control.

## Required sections

Each CFP publication must include:

- cover;
- publication information;
- executive summary;
- purpose and intended audience;
- main content;
- evidence of capacity;
- opportunities for cooperation or next steps, where relevant;
- methodological or scope note;
- version history;
- contact and stable publication URL.

## Claim standard

A current-achievement claim should be linked to at least one of the following:

- public webpage;
- repository path;
- published document identifier;
- dated screenshot or release record;
- verifiable project output;
- partner confirmation;
- official correspondence.

Planned outputs must be labelled as planned, proposed, in development or subject to funding.

## Tone

Use a calm, precise and partnership-oriented voice. Avoid superlatives unless they can be independently verified.

## Core descriptor

Use consistently:

> Civic Forensics — Independent Civic Intelligence Initiative

## Core positioning statement

> Building democratic resilience through evidence, technology and civic intelligence.
