## Executive Summary

Civic Forensics is an independent civic intelligence initiative developed in Bosnia and Herzegovina. It creates practical methods, open publications and AI-assisted tools that help citizens, journalists, civil society organisations and public-interest professionals examine official decisions, institutional responses, public statements, media reports and quantitative claims.

The initiative addresses a recurring democratic problem: information may be formally public while remaining difficult to understand, verify or use. Official communication can contain unsupported conclusions, incomplete evidence, procedural formalism, ambiguous responsibility or numerical claims presented without sufficient context. At the same time, artificial intelligence is entering public communication and public administration faster than most citizens can develop the literacy needed to assess its use.

Civic Forensics responds by converting complex public material into structured and reproducible oversight processes. Its analytical cycle is:

> **problem → causes → actors → evidence → analysis → intervention → result → learning**

The initiative does not prescribe political conclusions. It separates facts, legal rules, institutional claims, interpretation, inference and allegation. AI output is treated as analytical assistance rather than evidence or an authoritative legal conclusion.

The current ecosystem includes:

- bilingual civic-information portals;
- an open methodology repository;
- case-based analyses of public administration and institutional communication;
- an open AI prompt library;
- the Democratic Resilience & AI Literacy Program concept;
- the EU Compliance Watch publication system;
- the first EU Compliance Watch Deep Dive on Article 50 of the EU AI Act;
- research concepts and prototypes for a Civic Intelligence Dashboard;
- publication standards designed for transparent, versioned and reusable outputs.

Civic Forensics is now seeking dialogue and cooperation with organisations working on democratic resilience, civil society, public-administration reform, AI literacy, digital rights, media and information literacy, open government, transparency and responsible digital transformation.

The immediate development opportunity is a structured pilot that combines civic education, regulatory monitoring, open-source tools, case-based learning and publication infrastructure. The purpose of a first partnership is not to replace existing institutions or watchdog organisations, but to provide a reusable civic-intelligence layer that helps diverse actors convert public information into verifiable evidence and lawful follow-up.

## 1. Who We Are

### Mission

To strengthen citizens’ ability to examine public decisions and institutional claims through transparent methods, verifiable evidence, responsible use of AI and practical civic-oversight tools.

### Vision

Public institutions should be transparent not only because information is published, but because important claims, decisions and procedures can be independently understood, tested and traced to evidence.

### Institutional descriptor

**Civic Forensics — Independent Civic Intelligence Initiative**

This descriptor reflects the initiative’s current nature. Civic Forensics is not presented as a public authority, regulator, accredited research institute or registered international organisation. It is an independent public-interest initiative building an open methodology, publication infrastructure and practical civic-learning ecosystem.

### Core values

1. **Evidence before assurance.** Institutional confidence does not replace documentation.
2. **Verification before conclusion.** A plausible claim is not automatically a proven claim.
3. **Neutral method, public-interest purpose.** The same analytical standard applies regardless of institution, party, official or media source.
4. **Clear limits.** Unknown facts, alternative explanations and evidentiary gaps are stated explicitly.
5. **Responsible AI.** AI supports extraction, comparison and question generation; it does not become the source of truth.
6. **Reproducibility.** Readers should be able to understand how a conclusion was reached.
7. **Lawful civic action.** Analysis should connect to proportionate and lawful follow-up options.

## 2. The Challenge

### 2.1 Public information is often available but not usable

Citizens encounter administrative decisions, public notices, procurement information, financial reports, public-company statements and political claims that require legal, procedural, numerical or technical knowledge to interpret. Publication alone does not guarantee practical transparency.

### 2.2 Administrative formalism can simulate accountability

An institution may reply within a formal process while avoiding the central question. A response may cite competence, procedure or a general legal provision without connecting the answer to the requested evidence. Civic Forensics uses the term **administrative formalism** for practices where procedural form creates an appearance of accountability without resolving the substantive issue.

### 2.3 Quantitative claims can create false certainty

Percentages, rankings, totals, forecasts and performance indicators may appear objective while hiding the denominator, comparison group, methodology, missing population or distinction between activity and outcome. Civic Forensics treats this as **quantitative formalism** when numbers produce an appearance of proof without demonstrating the relevant public result.

### 2.4 AI creates a new civic-literacy gap

AI systems can help institutions communicate and process information, but they can also make responsibility less visible. Citizens need practical skills to identify AI involvement, assess disclosure, preserve evidence, verify sources and understand when human responsibility remains decisive.

### 2.5 European standards need translation into civic practice

EU regulations, guidance and public-administration standards are often discussed at policy level. Citizens and local civil society need operational explanations: what changed, who is responsible, which evidence should exist, which questions can be asked and how formal compliance can be distinguished from meaningful implementation.

## 3. Our Response

### 3.1 Democratic Resilience & AI Literacy Program

The Democratic Resilience & AI Literacy Program is the principal programme framework of Civic Forensics. It combines:

- democratic and civic education;
- media and information literacy;
- data and quantitative literacy;
- legal and administrative literacy;
- responsible AI literacy;
- evidence-based public oversight;
- open publication and learning infrastructure.

The programme converts real public documents and claims into structured learning cases. Users are guided to identify the claim, locate evidence, check institutional authority and procedure, test alternative explanations, state the limits of the available evidence and identify lawful follow-up mechanisms.

**Status:** Programme concept in active development. Existing methodologies, analyses, prompts, portals and publication systems provide operational components; a full structured academy and funded multi-partner pilot remain planned developments.

### 3.2 Civic Forensics methodology

The methodology organises analysis around six questions:

1. What exactly is being claimed or decided?
2. Which facts and evidence support the claim?
3. Which institution or official has legal and practical responsibility?
4. Which procedure, standard or rule applies?
5. What remains unverified or open to another explanation?
6. What proportionate follow-up can produce additional evidence or accountability?

### 3.3 Operational cycle

> **Evidence → Verification → Analysis → Publication → Civic Action → Institutional Response → Learning**

A publication is not treated as the end of the process. A new official response, court decision, inspection record or disclosed document becomes a new evidence input. Previous conclusions can then be marked as confirmed, amended, disproved or supplemented.

## 6. Publications

### 6.1 EU Compliance Watch

EU Compliance Watch translates European regulatory developments into practical and verifiable civic-oversight materials.

Its publication series are:

- **ECW-DD** — Deep Dive;
- **ECW-WR** — Watch Report;
- **ECW-CG** — Citizen Guide;
- **ECW-CL** — Citizen Checklist.

The system focuses on AI governance, data protection, transparency, public administration, digital government, public procurement, public money, cybersecurity, open data and institutional accountability.

### 6.2 First Deep Dive

**ECW-DD-001 — Article 50 Is Now Enforceable: A Civic Guide to AI Transparency**

**Status:** Published output, with final portal URL and repository release record to be confirmed in the final CFP-001 edition.

The publication explains the transparency obligations of Article 50 of the EU AI Act from the perspective of citizens, journalists, auditors and public authorities. It demonstrates the core Civic Forensics approach: moving from legal obligation to verifiable evidence, compliance indicators, red flags and practical questions.

### 6.3 Case studies and explanatory publications

**Status: Published and working outputs**

Civic Forensics develops case-based analyses concerning official decisions, institutional responses, local government, public services, public communication, quantitative claims and access to information.

Publication requires privacy protection, source preservation, legal-risk review and clear separation between proven facts, interpretation and allegation.

### 6.4 Field manual and methodological materials

**Status: Published output / edition records to be consolidated**

The initiative has developed a field-manual approach to AI and bureaucracy, supported by public methodologies and reusable prompts. The final CFP-001 edition should include complete bibliographic data and stable links for each edition cited.

## 8. Evidence of Capacity

Civic Forensics demonstrates capacity through working public outputs rather than institutional assurances alone.

### Evidence of Capacity — Methodological depth

The initiative has documented standards for analysing public decisions, institutional conduct, official statements and numerical claims. The methodology requires a chain from question to fact, evidence, rule, reasoning and conclusion.

### Evidence of Capacity — Open infrastructure

The public repository makes methods and development concepts visible. This permits inspection of how analytical tools evolve and supports future collaboration.

### Evidence of Capacity — Case-based practice

The methodology has been applied to real administrative and public-interest material. Case work has generated practical lessons concerning evidence preservation, access to information, institutional competence, legal procedure, quantitative claims and documented escalation.

### Evidence of Capacity — Publication discipline

EU Compliance Watch and CFP Publication Kit introduce document identifiers, metadata, versions, citation rules, status labels and evidence registers. This converts informal content production into a repeatable publication process.

### Evidence of Capacity — Integrated programme logic

Research, education, technology and civic action are not treated as separate projects. A regulatory analysis can generate a Citizen Guide, a checklist, a training exercise, a dashboard schema and a structured request for information.

## 9. Pilot Opportunity

### Proposed working title

**Democratic Resilience & AI Literacy Pilot: Civic Oversight in the Age of AI**

### Proposed duration

12 months, subject to partner consultation and available support.

### Geographic approach

Initial implementation in Bosnia and Herzegovina, with materials designed for regional and European reuse.

### Target groups

- citizens and community initiatives;
- civil society organisations;
- journalists and local media;
- students and educators;
- public-sector communication and transparency staff;
- legal, audit and public-administration professionals;
- local-government and public-service stakeholders.

### Proposed activities

1. Validate and adapt the Civic Forensics methodology with external experts.
2. Produce an English and local-language AI literacy and civic-oversight toolkit.
3. Develop a structured set of case-based learning modules.
4. Publish a regular EU Compliance Watch cycle.
5. Train a pilot group of users and facilitators.
6. Test evidence registers, checklists and open-source analytical prompts.
7. Develop and test a limited Civic Intelligence Dashboard prototype.
8. Document learning, corrections, safeguards and user feedback.
9. Produce an evaluation and sustainability plan.

### Proposed outputs

- 4 Deep Dive publications;
- 12 Watch Reports;
- 8 Citizen Guides;
- 12 Citizen Checklists;
- structured learning modules and exercises;
- facilitator materials;
- open-source prompt and evidence templates;
- pilot dashboard prototype;
- evaluation report and lessons-learned publication.

These figures are proposed pilot targets, not current achievements.

### Intended outcomes

- improved ability to distinguish facts, legal claims, policy messages and unsupported conclusions;
- increased practical AI literacy in public-administration and public-information contexts;
- stronger ability to identify and request missing evidence;
- improved quality of civic questions and lawful follow-up;
- reusable open materials for civil society and education;
- stronger translation of European standards into everyday civic practice.

### Safeguards

- political and institutional neutrality of the analytical method;
- explicit separation of fact, interpretation, inference and allegation;
- privacy protection and data minimisation;
- human editorial responsibility;
- source and evidence-gap disclosure;
- right of reply and correction procedures;
- legal-risk and safeguarding review;
- no automated legal determination or compliance verdict without evidence;
- transparent disclosure of AI assistance.

## 11. Sustainability

### Open materials

Core methodologies, prompts and publication standards are designed for open reuse under an appropriate non-commercial share-alike licence.

### Version-controlled knowledge base

GitHub serves as the development and evidence repository. Stable releases and source files can support transparent updates and correction history.

### Publication library

EU Compliance Watch creates a recurring output cycle that can continue beyond an individual training event or funded pilot.

### Training-of-trainers potential

Citizen Guides, checklists, exercises and facilitator notes can support local multipliers rather than reliance on one central trainer.

### Modular development

The programme can begin with publications and workshops and later add platform functions, structured courses and dashboard capabilities.

### Partnership diversity

Sustainability should not depend on a single donor. Relevant cooperation may include civil society, academia, public-interest technology, media, European networks and public-administration reform programmes.

### Governance requirement

Future expansion will require documented editorial governance, financial administration, data protection, safeguarding, security and conflict-of-interest procedures. These should be established before scaling.
