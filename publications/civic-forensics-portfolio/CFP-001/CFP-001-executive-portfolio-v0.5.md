## Executive Summary

Civic Forensics is an independent civic intelligence initiative developed in Bosnia and Herzegovina. It creates practical methods, open publications and AI-assisted tools that help citizens, journalists, civil society organisations and public-interest professionals examine official decisions, institutional responses, public statements, media reports and quantitative claims.

The initiative addresses a recurring democratic problem: information may be formally public while remaining difficult to understand, verify or use. Civic Forensics converts complex public material into structured and reproducible oversight processes:

> **Evidence → Verification → Analysis → Publication → Civic Action → Institutional Response → Learning**

The initiative separates facts, legal rules, institutional claims, interpretation, inference and allegation. AI output is treated as analytical assistance rather than evidence or an authoritative legal conclusion.

Civic Forensics operates complementary Serbian- and English-language platforms. The Serbian-language platform focuses on civic oversight, public accountability and citizen tools in Bosnia and Herzegovina. The English-language platform presents methodologies, publications and European regulatory analyses for an international audience.

The current ecosystem includes the foundational field manual *Vještačka inteligencija i birokratija*, the Civic Forensics AI Prompt Framework, EU Compliance Watch, the Democratic Resilience & AI Literacy Program, open repositories and research concepts for a Civic Intelligence Dashboard.

## 1. Why Civic Forensics

Public information is often available but not practically usable. Citizens encounter administrative decisions, procurement notices, public-company reports, urban-planning documents, institutional statements and numerical claims that require legal, procedural, financial or technical knowledge to interpret.

Administrative formalism can simulate accountability. Quantitative claims can create false certainty. Artificial intelligence creates a new civic-literacy gap. European standards therefore need translation into civic practice: what changed, who is responsible, which evidence should exist, which questions can be asked and how formal compliance can be distinguished from meaningful implementation.

## 2. Mission, Vision and Values

### Mission

To strengthen citizens’ ability to examine public decisions and institutional claims through transparent methods, verifiable evidence, responsible use of AI and practical civic-oversight tools.

### Vision

Public institutions should be transparent not only because information is published, but because important claims, decisions and procedures can be independently understood, tested and traced to evidence.

### Core values

1. Evidence before assurance.
2. Verification before conclusion.
3. Neutral method, public-interest purpose.
4. Clear limits and disclosed uncertainty.
5. Responsible AI with human accountability.
6. Reproducibility.
7. Lawful civic action.

## 4. Civic Forensics Methodology

The methodology organises analysis around six questions:

1. What exactly is being claimed or decided?
2. Which facts and evidence support the claim?
3. Which institution or official has legal and practical responsibility?
4. Which procedure, standard or rule applies?
5. What remains unverified or open to another explanation?
6. What proportionate follow-up can produce additional evidence or accountability?

A new official response, court decision, inspection record or disclosed document becomes a new evidence input. Previous conclusions can then be confirmed, amended, disproved or supplemented.

## 7. Civic Forensics AI Prompt Framework

### 7.1 Purpose

The Civic Forensics AI Prompt Framework is an open collection of structured methodologies for evidence-based and reproducible analysis of public information. It is intended for citizens, journalists, researchers, educators, civil society organisations and public-interest professionals.

The framework does not automate legal or political conclusions. It helps users identify claims, distinguish official information from independent confirmation, locate missing evidence, map institutional responsibility and formulate proportionate verification steps.

### 7.2 Methodological Principles

- evidence-based analysis;
- reproducibility;
- transparency and source disclosure;
- distinction between official information and independent confirmation;
- separation of fact, interpretation, inference and allegation;
- human accountability for final conclusions;
- explainable AI-assisted reasoning;
- data minimisation and privacy protection;
- openness to correction when new evidence becomes available.

### 7.3 Local Civic Prompt Suite — Serbian

| Prompt family | Purpose | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional PR Analysis | Tests whether institutional communication informs, promotes, minimises, shifts responsibility or creates formal transparency without verifiable evidence. | Published |
| Media Manipulation Analysis | Separates source claims, author wording, editorial amplification, clickbait, omissions and political steering. | Published |
| Political Statement Analysis — Election Context 2026 | Rapid analysis of political claims, implicit assumptions, persuasive techniques and evidence gaps. | Published |
| Quick Civic News Analysis | Fast review of public-interest news, legal context, evidence, deadlines, figures and administrative formalism. | Published |
| Statement Chain Analysis | Longitudinal tracking of claims, evidence, deadlines, responsibility and later corrections. | Published |

Each local prompt family includes a corresponding user guide.

### 7.4 EU Prompt Suite — English

| Prompt family | Purpose | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional PR Communication Analysis — EU Edition | Analyses communication by EU, national, regional and local authorities, public undertakings and public-fund managers. | Published |
| Media Manipulation Analysis — EU Edition | Reviews media framing, source attribution, political steering, EU competences and multilingual context. | Published |
| Rapid Forensic Analysis of a Political Statement — EU Electoral Context | Examines political claims across European, national, regional and local election contexts. | Published |
| Quick Citizen Analysis of News and Public Statements — EU Edition | Provides a citizen-oriented review of official claims, EU law, competences, funding stages and evidence gaps. | Published |
| Public Statement Analysis Protocol — EU Edition | Comprehensive claim-by-claim protocol for EU and Member State public statements. | Published |

Each EU prompt family includes a corresponding English-language user guide.

### 7.5 User Guides

The user guides explain when to use each prompt, the minimum input required, how to preserve source context, how to interpret classifications and scores, how to verify AI-generated citations and legal references, how to avoid treating missing evidence as proof of falsehood, how to protect personal and sensitive data and how to publish only manually verified findings.

### 7.6 Open Methodology

The framework is published as an open civic methodology intended for responsible use, adaptation, translation and reuse. Individual prompt files retain the licence and attribution notices contained in their published editions, primarily CC BY 4.0.

### 7.7 Future Development

Planned prompt families include AI Act compliance assessment, GDPR accountability, public procurement and public funds, DORA implementation, access to documents and open data, anti-corruption and conflict-of-interest analysis, multilingual editions and integration with the future Civic Intelligence Dashboard.
