# When Persuasive Text Is Not Evidence

As AI systems become better at producing fluent and convincing legal, administrative and civic analysis, a basic question becomes more important: **can we still tell what is actually supported by evidence, what comes from a source, what is an inference, and what remains only an assumption?**

The new **Civic Forensics White Paper 2026 — “When Persuasive Text Is Not Evidence”** addresses this problem through a methodological framework built around *Evidence Ceiling*, *Claim Drift* and *Claim Provenance*, supported by the control chain:

**CLAIM → SOURCE → STATUS → CONFIDENCE → ACTION**

The paper argues that **AI literacy should include evidence literacy**: the ability to distinguish sources, claims, inferences, uncertainty and missing evidence when working with AI-assisted analysis.

The central principle is simple: **better AI does not reduce the need for evidence discipline — it makes it more important.**
