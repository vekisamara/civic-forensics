# Title

## Executive summary

State the issue, the principal finding, the evidential strength and the practical significance in no more than 250 words.

## 1. Subject of analysis

Identify the decision, statement, institutional action, omission, report or procedure being examined.

## 2. Public-interest relevance

Explain which public right, public resource, institutional duty, affected group or democratic interest is engaged.

## 3. Jurisdiction and applicable framework

Identify:

- the competent jurisdiction;
- the authority or institution concerned;
- the applicable procedural framework;
- any relevant EU-law dimension;
- the available remedy or oversight route, if established.

Do not treat non-binding standards as enforceable law.

## 4. Method and analytical question

State the Civic Forensics method and version used. Formulate the central question as a verifiable question rather than an allegation.

## 5. Sources and evidence status

## 12. Alternative explanations

Identify reasonable explanations that could weaken or change the principal finding. State what evidence would confirm or reject them.

## 13. Legal and institutional analysis

Separate:

1. verified fact;
2. legal rule or standard;
3. interpretation;
4. inference;
5. unresolved legal question.

## 14. Material impact

Explain the practical legal, social, financial, environmental, administrative or rights-based effect. Distinguish administrative activity from actual protection or outcome.

## 16. Recommendations and next steps

Recommendations must be proportionate, lawful and connected to the evidence. Put time-limited legal remedies before optional mechanisms.

## 17. Right of reply

State whether the relevant institution or materially affected person was invited to comment, the date, the response received and any resulting correction.

## 18. Limitations

Identify unavailable documents, incomplete datasets, jurisdictional uncertainty, time constraints, source restrictions and unresolved questions.

## 19. Conclusion

State only what the evidence supports. Avoid attributing motive, intent, corruption, discrimination or guilt unless the required evidential threshold has been met.
